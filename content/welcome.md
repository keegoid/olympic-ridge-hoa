## What We're About

These are the things this neighborhood runs on. None of them are rules you have to look up — they are just how we treat each other and the place we share.

* **We look out for each other.** A neighbor's report about traffic on the access road is why that road has bollards today.
* **We drive like kids live here.** The community speed limit is 25 mph, and slower near the bus stop.
* **We keep a lane open.** One entire side of every street is a fire lane and stays clear so fire trucks, ambulances, and snow plows can get through.
* **We share the view.** Nobody's shed should cost a neighbor their mountains, which is why exterior projects go through architectural review first.
* **We clean up after ourselves.** Leash on when you're off your own property, bag in hand, and trash cans screened from the street.

## The County Doesn't Plow This Hill. We Do.

This is the part most residents never hear, and it explains almost everything about how the HOA works.

Olympic Ridge was developed and approved as a **self-sufficient community**. As a condition of that approval, Mason County does not maintain our roads, sidewalks, streetlights, or related infrastructure — the Association does. That responsibility cannot be handed back to the county. It is permanent, and it is why the Association keeps a reserve study that models 30 years of replacement costs.

<div class="content-callout">
  <strong>What we all chip in for:</strong>
  snow plowing; roads, sidewalks, and streetlights; groundskeeping of the common areas, now including the hill and the pond; a $7,500 brush cleanup; community insurance; and common-area repairs. Annual dues for 2026 are $1,000 per home, about $83 a month.
</div>

### Straight talk about the money

Reserves are behind where the reserve study says they should be. Rather than reach for a dues increase first, the Board moved $40,000 out of a non-interest-bearing checking account and into a certificate of deposit so that money earns interest instead of losing value to inflation. That interest is not profit paid to anyone — it offsets Association expenses.

No dues increase is proposed. A raise is the last resort, and any change would be presented with supporting figures before it was considered. The full picture is in the [meeting minutes](/minutes/).

## Three Things You Can Do

<div class="approval-path">
  <div class="step-card">
    <span class="step-number">1</span>
    <strong>Manage email notice consent</strong>
    <span>Washington law requires your written authorization before the Association can send you official notices by email. Only 8 of about 140 mailed forms came back, and mailing costs doubled against budget. The form lets you give consent, change your email, or revoke consent. New consents and email changes stay on paper until the Association verifies the owner and email address and J&amp;M confirms processing.</span>
  </div>
  <div class="step-card">
    <span class="step-number">2</span>
    <strong>Sit in on a meeting</strong>
    <span>Open board meetings are the last Saturday of each month at 9:00 AM Pacific by Google Meet, and any owner may attend to listen or raise an issue. Dates are on the community calendar. The Google Meet link is not published on this public site — it was mailed to owners with the 2026 meeting schedule and is posted in the private community Facebook group.</span>
  </div>
  <div class="step-card">
    <span class="step-number">3</span>
    <strong>Come to the block party</strong>
    <span>Our once-a-year neighborhood picnic and potluck happens every September at the main park off Belfair Station. Watch the announcements for this year's date.</span>
  </div>
