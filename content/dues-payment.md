## What You Owe

<div class="content-callout">
  <strong>2026 annual assessment: $1,000 per home</strong> — about $83 a month.
  This was set in the 2026 budget approved at the January 16, 2026 community meeting, an increase of
  $60 per year ($5 per month) from the previous $940 to cover rising utility and maintenance costs.
</div>

Assessments are what fund everything the Association is responsible for — snow plowing, the roads, sidewalks, and streetlights the county does not maintain, groundskeeping of the common areas, insurance, common-area repairs, and the reserve fund that pays to replace those assets as they wear out. There is more on where the money goes on the [welcome page](/welcome/) and in the published [meeting minutes](/minutes/).

## Who Bills You

Billing and payment processing are handled by the Association's property management company, **J&M Management**, not by the Board directly. J&M issues owner invoices and statements and maintains the account records.

If you did not receive an invoice or statement, the most likely cause is an out-of-date mailing address on file. Contact J&M Management to confirm you are on the correct mailing list, and consider [signing up for electronic notices](/email-consent/) so future statements reach you reliably.

## Your Rights on Payment

Washington's Uniform Common Interest Ownership Act applies to Olympic Ridge. Under [RCW 64.90.480](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=64.90.480), the Association must make at least one method of paying assessments available to owners **at no charge**, or treat the cost of that method as a common expense. If every payment option presented to you carries a fee, ask — one of them should not.

You are also entitled to a statement of your account. Budget materials generally include assessment amounts and due dates, reserve contributions, and reserve study status. See the [Washington compliance notes](/wa-compliance/#budgets-assessments--payments) for what to expect on budgets, assessments, and payments.

## If You Have Fallen Behind

Contact J&M Management or the Board before it escalates. Payment arrangements are routinely used and are a normal part of how associations handle this — several accounts in the community are already on them. Unpaid assessments accrue and can eventually become a lien against the property, which is worse for everyone than a conversation now.

Individual account details are confidential and are never discussed in the public portion of a board meeting or included in published minutes.
