## Quick Recap

The HOA Board held its regular monthly open board meeting at the Olympic Ridge main park, with remote access available through Google Meet, on August 29, 2026. The meeting began at approximately 9:04 AM and ran for more than two hours. It drew the largest turnout yet for an open Board meeting under the monthly schedule.

Homeowners contributed extensive comments, questions, and practical ideas throughout the meeting. The discussion covered Association finances, landscaping and Scotch broom control, parking and road safety, enforcement practices, architectural review, management services, and better ways to communicate with the community. The Board identified several ideas for follow-up, including road striping and no-parking zones, more visible information near the mailboxes, clearer homeowner guidance, and possible volunteer maintenance efforts.

## Board Direction and Action Record

The working notes did not record a formal roll-call vote during this meeting. They did record the following Board direction and completed work:

* **Certificate of deposit completed:** The Association completed the previously approved transfer of $40,000 into a six-month certificate of deposit so that available funds earn interest while remaining reserved for Association needs.
* **Fire-lane enforcement:** The Board indicated that fire-lane violations require prompt enforcement rather than repeated warnings, including towing when authorized and necessary to keep emergency access clear.
* **Bus stop and blind-corner safety:** The Board agreed to develop a project for no-parking areas near the bus stop, blind corners, and mailbox area, subject to confirming scope, markings, contractor availability, and cost.
* **Trash-container enforcement clarification:** The Board directed that enforcement distinguish between containers stored in front of a home and containers kept at the side, while continuing to require screening from street view under the governing documents.
* **CC&amp;R amendment planning:** Potential CC&amp;R changes will be collected into one coordinated package before legal review to reduce duplicated legal expense. Any amendment would still follow the approval process required by the governing documents and applicable law.
* **Community information access:** The Board supported adding an information box or similar public notice point near the community mailboxes, subject to a workable location and cost.

## Discussion Summary

### Financial Planning and Association Operations

The Board reported that the previously approved $40,000 certificate of deposit has been established for a six-month term. The investment allows Association funds to earn interest instead of remaining entirely in non-interest-bearing accounts. The Board also noted that common-area property taxes are being included in 2027 planning and that future budget presentations should make long-term reserve needs easier for homeowners to understand.

Rising fuel, labor, material, insurance, mailing, and infrastructure costs were discussed. A possible annual adjustment in the range of inflation was mentioned as part of long-term planning, but the working notes do not record the Board adopting a 2027 assessment amount or approving a dues increase at this meeting. The Board plans to present supporting financial information before future budget decisions.

Homeowners asked about the cost and value of the management company. The Board explained that management provides accounting, administrative, inspection, and related support that would be difficult for a volunteer Board to replace directly. The Board will continue monitoring service quality and following up when management work needs correction.

### Landscaping, Common Areas, and Scotch Broom

The Board reviewed recent Scotch broom removal and other cleanup work along the cul-de-sac and access road. Additional pond-area work is planned for the cooler season, when removal is expected to be more practical and cost-effective.

Homeowners proposed preventive maintenance before Scotch broom matures and becomes more expensive to remove. Ideas included periodic landscaping-contract work and organized volunteer cleanup. The Board will explore a repeatable schedule and determine which areas and tasks can be handled safely and within the budget.

The Board also discussed uncertainty inherited from the original development records concerning some road and parcel boundaries. Work continues to identify Association-owned parcels and clarify maintenance responsibility.

### Parking, Traffic, and Road Safety

Parking and speeding generated substantial homeowner comment. Concerns included blocked fire lanes, parking near the school bus stop and blind corners, leaking vehicles that may damage Association roads, and speeding on Ridge Point Boulevard.

The Board emphasized that fire lanes must remain clear and indicated that immediate enforcement, including towing when authorized, may be necessary for safety. For other recurring parking issues, the Board discussed clearer procedures and markings that make restrictions easier to understand and enforce.

Potential improvements included red curb markings, no-parking zones near the bus stop and mailbox area, stop lines, speed-limit markings painted on the road, and additional signs where warranted. Speed bumps were considered impractical because of snow removal and maintenance concerns. Cameras and vehicle registration were raised as ideas, but both would require further legal, privacy, operational, and cost review. No camera or vehicle-registration program was approved at this meeting.

### Property Maintenance and Enforcement

Homeowners discussed lawn care, weeds and overgrowth, trash-container screening, vehicle leaks, inspection timing, and the cost of repeated enforcement notices. The Board acknowledged the need for consistent enforcement while also recognizing drought conditions, differing household resources, and opportunities for neighbors to help one another.

The discussion distinguished normal seasonal lawn discoloration from weeds or overgrowth that may violate the governing documents. The Board will consider publishing practical lawn-care information as a community resource. General guidance does not replace the governing documents, the inspection process, or an ACC request where one is required.

The Board also clarified that homeowners who believe a warning was issued in error should contact the Board for review. Property-specific enforcement matters remain private and are not included in these minutes.

### Architectural Review and Governing Documents

The Board reminded homeowners to submit an ACC request before making structural changes or material changes to an exterior or landscape design. Routine planting within an existing bed was discussed as different from changing the design or structure of the property. Obtaining a written decision creates a durable record for the homeowner and the Association.

Homeowners also discussed possible future changes to the CC&amp;Rs. The Board plans to collect proposals into one package so the community can evaluate them together and legal review does not have to be repeated for isolated changes. No CC&amp;R amendment was adopted at this meeting.

### Communication and Community Participation

Homeowners asked for more visible meeting notices and easier access to Association information. The Board reiterated that the website is the public source for meeting records, governing documents, forms, announcements, and the Board contact form. Messages submitted through the website go to the Board.

The Board discussed adding an information box or notice point near the mailboxes and distributing printed information to households. A voluntary resident directory was also suggested for emergencies and neighbor-to-neighbor help. Any directory would require affirmative participation and privacy safeguards; no resident directory was approved or created at this meeting.

The Board encouraged owners who want email delivery of official notices to use the Association's consent process. A submitted request remains subject to owner, email, and management verification before official delivery changes from paper to email.

### Community Ideas and Participation

The large turnout produced many constructive ideas, including preventive Scotch broom work, volunteer yard help, better safety markings, improved homeowner education, and additional ways to reach residents who do not use social media. The Board thanked homeowners for participating and will continue evaluating the suggestions recorded above based on legal requirements, governing documents, safety, cost, and available volunteer capacity.

Future regular open Board meetings will continue on the last Saturday of each month at 9:00 AM Pacific. During the colder season, meetings will be held remotely rather than in the park. Owners should use the community calendar for meeting details and contact the Board if they need access information.
